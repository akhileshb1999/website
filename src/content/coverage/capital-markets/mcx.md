• MCX holds a near-monopoly in Indian commodity derivatives and is the largest commodity options exchange globally, which gives it the same zero-marginal-cost economics as any exchange. FY26 demonstrated what that means when volumes surge: EBITDA margin expanded from 63% to 73% and PAT rose 138% to ₹1,332cr on total income up 101%. Margin expanding ten points in a single year is not operational improvement — it is a fixed cost base being spread across far more trades.

• The composition of that growth is the critical caveat. Options notional average daily turnover more than doubled to ₹4,71,641cr and options revenue reached ₹1,398cr against futures at ₹693cr, so options now generate twice the revenue of futures. But the volume surge sits substantially in bullion, which means a meaningful part of FY26 earnings reflects where gold and silver prices were rather than structural growth in participation [bullion share of turnover: __%; volume growth ex-bullion: __%].

• Product expansion is the diversification answer — electricity futures, BULLDEX options, cardamom, nickel, gold ten and monthly bullion options — and it matters because a commodity exchange concentrated in one complex inherits that complex's cycle. Whether new contracts build durable liquidity rather than launching and fading is the measurable test [non-bullion share of turnover FY26: __% -> FY29E: __%].

• Key risk: 1) Commodity price cycles reversing, which would deflate the volume base and reverse operating leverage as sharply as it worked in FY26 2) Regulatory changes to position limits or product approvals, in a market where SEBI controls what can be listed and on what terms.
