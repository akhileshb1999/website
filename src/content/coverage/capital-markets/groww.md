• Groww earns a net margin near 45% — FY26 PAT of ₹2,083cr on revenue of ₹4,645cr — roughly two and a half times Angel One's on comparable revenue. The difference is structural rather than operational: Groww acquired its user base through an app-first product with organic, low-cost acquisition, carrying no branch network, no sub-broker chain and no legacy distribution to fund. In a business where the product is undifferentiated and price competition has driven brokerage toward zero, cost of acquisition and cost to serve are the only durable advantages.

• The concern is where growth now comes from. Groww is the largest broker by NSE active clients, but that base was essentially flat year on year, so FY26 revenue growth of 19% and Q4 revenue growth of 88% came from monetisation per client rather than from adding clients. • The regulatory backdrop is the dominant variable and it is not company-specific. SEBI's tightening of derivatives rules cut industry F&O volumes materially, and Groww's revenue is concentrated in a segment the regulator has stated an intention to moderate. The 67% Q4 EBITDA margin is what this model produces in favourable conditions; the relevant question is what it produces after the next intervention [F&O share of revenue: __%].

• Key risk: 1) Further SEBI restriction on retail derivatives participation, which strikes directly at the revenue pool 2) A flat active client base leaving growth dependent on monetisation intensity in a category where competitors compete on price.
