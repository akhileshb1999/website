• Exchange economics are close to the best in any industry: the marginal cost of processing an additional trade is effectively zero, so incremental volume converts almost entirely into profit. BSE's FY26 operating EBITDA margin of 63% excluding the Core SGF is that structure made visible. The corollary is that the business is a pure volume proxy — transaction charges contributed ₹3,795cr of ₹4,834cr of revenue, roughly 78%, growing 87%, leaving the company almost wholly dependent on derivatives activity it does not control.

• The market share question needs the right denominator to be meaningful. BSE has closed the gap dramatically on notional turnover, but revenue accrues on premium turnover, where NSE retains roughly 74.7% share, alongside ~93% of the cash market and ~99.8% of equity futures. Notional share is the headline; premium share is the earnings. Any thesis on BSE's convergence has to be argued on premium capture rather than on notional [BSE premium turnover share FY26: __% -> FY29E: __%].

• Volume itself has been extraordinary — average daily notional turnover in equity derivatives reached ₹245 trillion in Q4 FY26 against ₹112 trillion a year earlier — but composition matters, because proprietary and algorithmic flow dominates derivatives turnover and responds sharply to changes in cost of capital and margin rules. Regulatory intervention in this segment has repeatedly reset volumes without notice [proprietary share of turnover: __%; retail participation: __%].

• Key risk: 1) SEBI action on derivatives — position limits, expiry-day rules, lot sizes or charge structures — which can remove a large share of volume by administrative decision 2) Dependence on a single revenue line, with ~78% of revenue tied to transaction charges on activity outside the company's control.
