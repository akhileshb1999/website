• Angel One sits between two models and carries the cost of both: it has scaled a digital client base to 37.4 million with 16.7% demat market share, but its economics still reflect a heavier cost structure than the app-native competition. FY26 makes the comparison stark — PAT of ₹915cr on total income of ₹5,152cr is a net margin near 18%, against Groww earning ₹2,083cr on ₹4,645cr. Similar revenue scale, roughly a third of the profit.

• FY26 profit fell 21.9%, and the cause was regulatory rather than competitive: SEBI's derivatives tightening compressed industry F&O volumes, and Angel One's revenue mix left it more exposed than peers. That is the defining characteristic of the broking model in India — earnings are a function of retail speculative activity, which the regulator has explicitly set out to moderate [F&O share of revenue: __%; FY29E net margin: __%].

• The strategic response is diversification into asset management and wealth, where wealth AUM stood at ₹100.8bn and AMC AUM at ₹3.6bn. These are structurally better businesses — recurring, fee-based and less regulator-sensitive than transaction revenue — but they are small relative to the broking base, so the question is whether they scale fast enough to change the earnings profile before the next regulatory cycle [wealth and AMC share of revenue FY26: __% -> FY29E: __%].
