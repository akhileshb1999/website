• Blue Dart is the premium air express incumbent — DHL-owned, operating its own aircraft fleet, serving time-definite B2B delivery where reliability is worth paying for. That was a strong position when speed required air. It is a harder one now that surface express networks have compressed transit times at materially lower cost, leaving Blue Dart defending a premium against competitors converging on its service level from below.

• The growth gap makes the pressure explicit: FY26 revenue grew 7.35% to ₹6,141cr while Delhivery grew 17.6% and Shadowfax 66.4%. Profit fell 2.02% to ₹240cr, and operating margin compressed 290bps in a single quarter, from 17.38% in December 2025 to 14.48% in March 2026, with FY26 EBITDA margin at 10.44% [FY29E EBITDA margin: __%; yield per shipment: __].

• The cost structure is the vulnerability in a decelerating volume environment. An owned aircraft fleet is a fixed cost that requires throughput to justify, and the new Labour Codes added ₹443.6mn of exceptional charges in FY26 through higher employee benefits and handling costs. Fixed-cost intensity amplifies outcomes in both directions, and volumes are currently on the wrong side of it [aircraft utilisation: __; fixed cost as % of total: __].

• Key risk: 1) Structural erosion of the air express premium as surface networks close the transit-time gap at lower price points 2) Operating leverage working against the company, where a high fixed cost base compounds margin pressure when volume growth lags the industry.
