• Logistics is a density business: the cost of moving a parcel falls as more parcels move through the same sortation and line-haul network, which makes scale not merely an advantage but the entire economic model. Delhivery's response to a market competing on price was to buy capacity out of it — acquiring Ecom Express for ₹1,407cr in July 2025, then winding down its non-express operations and retaining only seven facilities. That is a consolidation trade rather than a revenue acquisition, and it should be judged on network cost per shipment rather than on acquired revenue.

• The margin evidence supports the density thesis: adjusted EBITDA margin rose from 1.7% in FY25 to 4.4% in FY26, with EBITDA up 70.3% against revenue growth of 17.6%. Reported net profit fell 5.9% to ₹153cr because integration costs sit below EBITDA, so the underlying operating improvement is masked at the profit line [FY29E adjusted EBITDA margin: __%; cost per shipment: __].

• Volume growth confirms the scale shift — one billion e-commerce shipments in FY26, equal to the company's entire first decade, with express parcel volumes up 72% to 306mn in Q4, though part of that reflects Ecom volumes migrating onto the network rather than organic share gain. • Key risk: 1) Quick commerce absorbing e-commerce demand into hyperlocal networks that bypass national parcel infrastructure entirely 2) Captive logistics arms at large e-commerce platforms internalising volume, alongside new Labour Codes raising the cost base across the industry.
