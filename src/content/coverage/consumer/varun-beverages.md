• Varun Beverages is PepsiCo's bottler rather than a brand owner, which means the moat is physical rather than intangible: route-to-market density, chilling infrastructure and distribution reach that a competitor must rebuild territory by territory. The most important development for the business was structural rather than operational — the exclusive bottling appointment with PepsiCo was extended to April 2049 and the previous special-purpose-vehicle restrictions removed, converting the one risk that could permanently impair the franchise into a twenty-three year runway with greater operating latitude.

• Margin is unusually high for a bottler at 27.7% EBITDA, built on India scale and backward integration, and the notable feature is that it is being deliberately diluted rather than defended: margin contracted 76bps as the lower-margin Twizza business in South Africa was consolidated. Growth and margin therefore trade off directly, and the question is whether international assets converge toward Indian margins over time or permanently reset the blended level lower [FY29E consolidated EBITDA margin: __%; international EBITDA margin FY26: __%].

• The growth algorithm is increasingly international: Q2 CY26 volumes grew 19.8% to 466.7mn cases, with India up 14.4% and international up 38.4%, and the Kenya asset acquisition adding further African reach. Crucially this growth is capital-light in the near term — the company carries roughly 50% more capacity than current volumes and has guided to no major India capex this year, so incremental volume converts at high incremental margin without a fresh capital cycle [incremental EBITDA margin on spare capacity: __%; Africa share of volumes: __% -> FY29E: __%].
