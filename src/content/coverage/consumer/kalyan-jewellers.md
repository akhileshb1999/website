• Kalyan's differentiator is not brand or merchandise but the balance sheet structure of its expansion. Jewellery retail is constrained by working capital, because inventory is gold and every new showroom requires a large upfront metal investment. The franchise-owned-company-operated model shifts that burden to the franchisee: FOCO showrooms now account for roughly half of revenue, letting the network expand at a pace the company's own balance sheet could not have funded.

• The trade-off is margin per rupee of revenue, and the numbers make it explicit: EBITDA margin of 6.3% in FY26 (7.2% in Q4) sits well below Titan's jewellery economics, because the franchisee takes a share of the value chain. The investment question is whether capital-light growth at 6-7% margins compounds returns better than owned growth at higher margins — a return-on-capital question rather than a margin question [FY29E EBITDA margin: __%; FY29E ROCE: __%].

• Balance sheet repair has run alongside the expansion: ₹6,461cr of working capital loans repaid in India since April 2023, with 40-50% of profits earmarked for debt reduction and shareholder returns. That discipline is what makes the FOCO model credible — expansion funded by franchisees rather than leverage — and the durability of the combination is what to track [net debt trajectory FY27E-29E: __; FOCO share of showrooms: __% -> __%].

• Key risk: 1) Franchisee economics deteriorating if gold prices or demand turn, since the model depends on partners willing to fund inventory 2) Competing directly with Titan for the same formalisation opportunity from a materially lower margin base.
