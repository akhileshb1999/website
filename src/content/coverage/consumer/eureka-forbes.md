• Eureka Forbes is best understood as an installed-base business rather than an appliance manufacturer. Selling a water purifier is a competitive, one-time, low-margin transaction; the value sits in what follows — filter replacements, annual maintenance contracts and service on units already in homes. That installed base is the asset, it is difficult for a competitor to attack because the switching decision only arises at replacement, and it produces recurring high-margin revenue that does not depend on winning each new sale.

• The margin trajectory reflects this shift: FY26 EBITDA margin of 12.2% marked a third consecutive year of improvement, on revenue of ₹2,710cr growing 11.3%. Under Advent's ownership since 2022 the company has been reorganising toward service monetisation, and the FY30 ambition — roughly doubling revenue to ₹5,400-5,600cr while tripling EBITDA to ₹800-850cr — implies margin expanding toward ~15%, which can only come from service and consumables mix rather than from hardware [service and consumables share of revenue FY26: __% -> FY29E: __%].

• The growth algorithm therefore has two separable parts: new unit placements, which expand the future annuity but carry low margin today, and monetisation intensity on existing units, which carries high margin and requires no new customer acquisition. The second is where the operating leverage sits, and where execution is genuinely measurable [filter replacement attach rate: __%; AMC penetration of installed base: __%].

• Key risk: 1) The FY30 targets implying a step-change in margin that depends on service attach rates not yet demonstrated at scale 2) Competitive intensity in new purifier sales from lower-priced entrants, which erodes the rate at which the installed base grows.
