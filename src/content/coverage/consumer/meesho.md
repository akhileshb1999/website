• Meesho is structurally different from Amazon and Flipkart rather than a smaller version of them: it charges sellers no commission and monetises through advertising and logistics, which lets it serve unbranded, low-ticket, price-led demand in tier-2 and beyond that commission-based marketplaces cannot economically service. The consequence is enormous order volume at very thin per-order economics — the business is a density and cost-per-shipment problem, not a take-rate problem.

• Unit economics have been improving on a measurable trajectory: contribution margin rose from 2.94% of NMV in FY23 to 4.95% in FY25, and free cash flow turned from -₹2,336cr in FY23 to +₹580cr in the twelve months to September 2025. At roughly ₹8 of contribution margin per order, profitability is a function of order volume and logistics cost per shipment rather than pricing [contribution margin % of NMV FY29E: __%; cost per shipment: __].

• The loss is narrowing rapidly rather than persisting structurally: FY26 revenue grew 34.5% to ₹12,626cr with a loss of ₹1,358cr, but the Q4 loss was only ₹166cr, down 88% year on year, on revenue up 47%. That trajectory implies breakeven is near, and the question shifts from whether the model works to what steady-state margin a zero-commission marketplace can sustain once growth investment normalises [FY29E EBITDA margin: __%].

• Key risk: 1) Competitive response from Amazon and Flipkart in the value segment, where both can subsidise longer than Meesho can 2) An unbranded, price-led customer base offering little pricing power, capping the steady-state take rate.
