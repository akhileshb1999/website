• Bajaj Consumer Care is effectively a single-brand business: Bajaj Almond Drops holds over 63% of the light hair oil category, dominance so complete that incremental share gain is no longer a meaningful growth lever. The moat is real — brand recall and distribution in a habit-driven category — but it is a moat around a narrow pond. Growth must come either from expanding light hair oil penetration itself or from establishing credibility in adjacent categories, and the latter has historically been the harder problem for this company.

• The FY26 margin move shows how much operating leverage sits on this cost base once volumes return: Q4 EBITDA grew 139.6% against revenue growth of 30.4%, and full-year PAT rose 51.8% on 21.4% revenue growth. That asymmetry is the signature of a fixed-cost-heavy single-brand structure — it cuts sharply in both directions, and the same leverage that produced FY26 would amplify any volume disappointment [FY26 EBITDA margin: __% -> FY29E: __%].

• The growth question is whether the revival is cyclical or structural. Absent category expansion or a second brand at scale, the algorithm is capped by light hair oil penetration.
