• Travel Food Services operates a concession business rather than a restaurant business, and the distinction determines everything about it: airports award food and lounge concessions through competitive tenders, so the asset is not brand equity but the contract portfolio and the operating capability that wins renewals. With around 30% of the Indian airport travel QSR market and roughly 45% of the airport lounge market, TFS is the incumbent in a structure where a captive customer, high dwell time and no price comparison produce materially better economics than street-side food service.

• Growth is a function of airport capacity expansion and passenger throughput rather than same-store demand, which makes this a leveraged play on Indian aviation infrastructure. FY26 system-wide sales grew 25.4% to ₹3,214cr with PAT up 21.5%, achieved despite subdued passenger traffic, and the network expanded by 76 QSR outlets and two lounges to more than 550 across 20 airports [outlets FY29E: __; sales per outlet: __].

• The structural vulnerability sits in the concession model itself: contracts are finite, renewals are competitive, and airport operators capture value through minimum guarantees and revenue shares.
