• Beer is structurally a different business from spirits: low value-to-weight makes freight prohibitive, so it must be brewed close to the market, which means UBL's economics are set state-by-state rather than nationally. FY26 shows this literally — Q4 sell-in volumes grew 4.1% with Andhra Pradesh, Assam and Maharashtra up while Rajasthan, Telangana and Orissa declined. The moat is the brewery footprint plus Kingfisher's shelf position; the constraint is that every state is effectively a separate regulatory market with its own pricing and distribution regime.

• Karnataka's move to Alcohol-in-Beverage-based excise is the first structural reform that taxes by alcohol content rather than volume, lowering the burden on beer relative to spirits. If that model spreads, it re-rates category economics in a way no amount of company execution could achieve [states adopting AIB by FY29E: __]. This matters more to the terminal margin than any internal initiative, and is the single most important thing to track.

• The margin picture is currently contradictory and worth resolving: gross margin expanded 222bps to 45.3%, yet Q4 operating margin of 6.19% was a seven-quarter low — meaning the entire gross margin gain, and more, was absorbed below the gross line. Management has flagged ₹400-500cr of cost headwinds tied to Middle East disruption, and premium volumes grew 16% in Q4 against total volumes of 4.1%, so mix is working while the cost base is not [FY29E EBITDA margin: __%; premium share of volumes FY26: __% -> FY29E: __%]. FY27 volume guidance is 6-7%.

• Key risk: 1) State-level policy reversals in the geographies currently driving growth, given how concentrated the volume swings are 2) Cost inflation continuing to absorb gross margin gains, as in FY26.
