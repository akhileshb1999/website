• Tata Consumer is best understood as a distribution asset rather than a portfolio of brands: tea and salt reach a very large share of Indian households but are commodity-adjacent categories with limited pricing power, and the value creation comes from what can be pushed through that same rail at higher margin. Underlying volume growth of 13% for the India branded business in FY26 indicates the rail is genuinely expanding rather than being priced up — the distinction that matters in a sector where much reported growth has been inflation pass-through.

• Margin here is a mix question. Tea behaves as pass-through — India tea volumes grew 4% in Q4 while revenue marginally declined, because lower input costs were handed to consumers — so beverages contribute reach, not profit growth. Foods is where the margin sits, and it is growing more than twice as fast (India Foods +18% in FY26 against India Beverages +8%). The margin trajectory is mechanically a function of how quickly foods outgrows beverages in the mix [foods share of India revenue FY26: __% -> FY29E: __%; India EBIT margin: __% -> __%].

• The growth algorithm is the scale-up of the acquired and incubated portfolio: growth businesses (Sampann, Capital Foods, Organic India, RTD) crossed ₹4,000cr in FY26 and now account for 31% of the India business, with Sampann alone growing 69% in Q4. This is the part that compounds — legacy tea and salt provide the distribution and the cash, while the growth portfolio determines the terminal margin [growth businesses share FY26: 31% -> FY29E: __%; revenue CAGR FY26-29E: __%].

• Key risk: 1) Integration and goodwill risk from a growth portfolio assembled largely through acquisition rather than organic build 2) Tea cost cycle reversing, compressing beverages margin without a corresponding pricing lever.
