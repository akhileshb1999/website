• Tilaknagar was, until recently, a regionally concentrated brandy specialist: Mansion House is India's largest prestige-and-above brandy at 10mn cases in FY26, up from 8.7mn in FY25. That leadership is genuine, but brandy is structurally a South Indian category, which left the business concentrated in precisely the states where route-to-market is most tightly controlled and where a single state's policy change can move the whole year. The Imperial Blue acquisition is the company's answer to that concentration.

• The acquisition transforms the shape of the business rather than merely adding to it: ₹3,442cr paid to Pernod Ricard in December 2025 buys national whisky distribution and takes P&A above 90% of volumes. FY26 revenue grew 69.9% to ₹2,346cr with only four months of Imperial Blue consolidated, so FY27 is the first clean year and reported growth until then is not comparable [FY27E revenue with full-year Imperial Blue: __; FY27E EBITDA margin: __%].

• The central tension is the balance sheet. The company spent FY20-FY25 reducing debt to a net cash position and cutting finance costs — the discipline that made it investable — and then committed ₹3,442cr, roughly 1.5x FY26 revenue, to a single acquisition. Management targets Net Debt/EBITDA below 1.0x by FY29 alongside 150-250bps of EBITDA margin expansion over 24-36 months while stepping up A&SP. Whether those two commitments are simultaneously achievable is the question the next eight quarters answer [Net Debt/EBITDA FY27E: __x -> FY29E: __x].

• Key risk: 1) Integration risk on an acquisition large relative to the acquirer, with the deleveraging path dependent on Imperial Blue delivering to plan 2) Brandy's regional concentration leaving the legacy business exposed to single-state policy action in the South.
