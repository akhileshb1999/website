• Vadilal is the clearest demonstration that ice cream economics depend on execution rather than category position: it holds roughly 15-20% of an organised Indian ice cream market that Amul leads with 40-45%, yet delivered an FY26 EBITDA margin of ~18% (operating profit ₹270cr on revenue ₹1,503cr) — materially ahead of the demerged Kwality Wall's despite the latter owning globally premium brands. Cold chain ownership and distribution control, not brand tier, are what determine profitability here.

• The margin structure is the whole thesis: a PAT margin of 10.3% in FY26 is exceptional for a business selling a frozen, perishable, seasonal product where distribution costs are high and shelf space is contested. This suggests either a genuinely superior cold chain cost position or a favourable regional concentration — distinguishing between the two matters, because the first scales nationally and the second does not [gross margin FY26: __%; cold chain cost as % of sales: __%].

• Growth momentum has accelerated sharply, with Q1 FY27 revenue up 34.2% and PAT up 96%, though a single quarter in the peak summer season is not a trend in a category this seasonal [FY27E revenue growth ex-seasonality: __%; FY29E EBITDA margin: __%]. The structural tailwind is the organised segment expanding as cold chain penetration deepens, which favours players with owned rather than rented distribution.

• Key risk: 1) Seasonality and weather dependence, with a disproportionate share of earnings concentrated in one quarter 2) Competitive intensity rising as Kwality Wall's operates standalone with freedom to invest aggressively for share.
