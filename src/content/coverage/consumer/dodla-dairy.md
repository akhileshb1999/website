• Dodla operates the same South Indian dairy model as Hatsun but at an earlier point in the value-added transition, and the numbers show what that costs: value-added products contributed ₹297cr of FY26 revenue of ₹4,125cr — roughly 7% — leaving the business predominantly liquid milk, which is close to a commodity. Procurement scale is genuine, with milk procurement up 13.4% and sales volumes at a record 14.0 LLPD, but scale in liquid milk buys throughput rather than margin.

• The margin consequence is direct: FY26 EBITDA margin of 7.5% sits well below Hatsun's ~12.0%, and that gap is the investment case expressed in a single number — it quantifies what a mix shift would be worth if the company can execute it [VAP share FY26: ~7% -> FY29E: __%; FY29E EBITDA margin: __%]. Revenue grew 11% while PAT rose only 2.7%, with elevated procurement costs absorbing the difference, illustrating how little buffer exists at this margin level.

• Growth is currently volume and geography led rather than mix led — milk sales volumes rose 19.5% and a Maharashtra plant is planned, extending beyond the southern base. Geographic expansion adds scale but replicates the same low-margin liquid milk model unless the value-added portfolio scales alongside it [revenue CAGR FY26-29E: __%].

• Key risk: 1) Procurement cost inflation against a 7.5% EBITDA margin that leaves almost no absorption capacity 2) Geographic expansion diluting returns if it extends the liquid milk mix rather than the value-added one.
