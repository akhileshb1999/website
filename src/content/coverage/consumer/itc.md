• ITC holds ~73% of India's legal cigarette market, in a category where advertising is banned outright — which makes incumbency close to unassailable, since a challenger cannot buy awareness and must rely on distribution ITC has spent decades building. The structural anomaly is that legal cigarettes are only ~10% of India's tobacco consumption against a ~90% global average, yet duty-paid volumes contribute over 80% of the government's tobacco tax revenue. The legal industry is taxed as though it were the whole market while competing against an untaxed one, and that asymmetry — not competition — is what governs volumes.

• Cigarette earnings are a function of the taxation cycle rather than operating leverage: FY26 cigarette revenue grew 13.7% but segment results only 5.1%, the gap being pass-through lag on higher levies. Genuine operating leverage sits in FMCG-Others, where EBITDA margin expanded ~200bps YoY to ~11% in Q4 FY26 (ex-Sresta) on a base that scaled from ~₹14,700cr in FY21 to ~₹24,200cr in FY26. The question is how far that converges toward established FMCG peers, since 100bps on this base now moves consolidated earnings more than a comparable move in cigarettes [FMCG-Others EBITDA margin FY26: ~11% -> FY29E: __%].

• Growth is value-led in cigarettes and volume-led in FMCG. GST on cigarettes moved from 28% to a flat 40% effective 1 Feb 2026, making FY27 the test of whether price increases hold without pushing volume into the illicit channel [FY27E cigarette volume: __%]. Everything else is cyclical rather than compounding — Paperboards is import-price dependent, Agri is exposed to trade flows — so the durable algorithm is cigarette pricing funding FMCG scale-up [blended revenue CAGR FY26-29E: __%].

• Key risk: 1) Each tax escalation historically shifts volume to illicit trade, now 26.1% of the market against 12.6% in 2012 2) FMCG margin convergence stalling if reinvestment intensity rises to defend share against regional challengers.
