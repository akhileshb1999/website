• Radico shows the cleanest evidence that premiumisation is working rather than being asserted: P&A accounts for 45.6% of IMFL volumes but contributes over 70% of IMFL revenue, implying realisation per case in P&A roughly three times the popular portfolio. In an industry where pricing is set by states and volume growth is structurally mid-single-digit, that realisation gap is the only meaningful compounding mechanism available, and Radico's position in white spirits — Magic Moments at ~8.6mn cases — gives it a category where it is the incumbent rather than a challenger.

• The margin proof is the gap between revenue and profit growth: FY26 EBITDA rose 52.4% against revenue growth of 24.7%, which is mix-driven operating leverage rather than cost reduction. The caveat is A&SP falling to 6.7% of IMFL sales in Q4 from 7.6%, so some of the near-term expansion is spend timing [FY29E EBITDA margin with normalised A&SP: __%]. Management guides to 125bps of expansion in FY27 from the ~16.8% base.

• The luxury portfolio is the option embedded in this business rather than the current earnings driver: Rampur single malt, Jaisalmer gin and Royal Ranthambore together delivered ₹475cr. Indian single malt is the rare Indian consumer category that commands global pricing and where brand equity compounds instead of being competed away, but it is small enough today that it changes valuation only if it scales [luxury as % of revenue FY26: __% -> FY29E: __%; revenue CAGR FY26-29E: __%].

• Key risk: 1) ENA and grain cost inflation, which compresses margin with no pricing recourse under state-controlled MRP 2) Competitive intensity rising in premium whisky and white spirits as every listed player pursues the same mix shift.
