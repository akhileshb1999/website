• DMart's entire edge is structural cost advantage, not merchandising: it owns rather than leases most stores, clusters them for supply-chain density, and deliberately restricts SKU count to concentrate purchase volumes per vendor, passing the resulting procurement discount into price. The consequence is that DMart's economics are driven by throughput per store rather than gross margin — it runs one of the thinnest gross margins in Indian retail and monetises through inventory turns. This is why the format compounds in value-conscious, non-metro catchments, and why competitors matching the price point without the ownership model bleed cash.

• That structure leaves almost no absorption capacity, which is what makes current cost pressure matter more than the magnitude suggests. EBITDA margin drifted from 7.9% to 7.8% in FY26 and PAT margin compressed 25bps to 4.81% as employee and finance costs outgrew gross profit. • The central question is whether quick commerce structurally impairs the model or merely trims it. Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart have absorbed the high-frequency top-up trip, which is the footfall driver for metro stores.
