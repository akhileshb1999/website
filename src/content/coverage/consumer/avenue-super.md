• DMart's entire edge is structural cost advantage: it owns rather than leases most stores, clusters them for supply-chain density, and deliberately restricts SKU count to concentrate purchase volumes per vendor, passing the resulting procurement discount into price. The consequence is that DMart's economics are driven by its high throughput per store (highest in industry). This is why the format compounds in value-conscious, non-metro catchments, and why competitors matching the price point without the ownership model bleed cash.

• The central question is whether quick commerce structurally impairs the model or merely trims it. Blinkit, Zepto and Instamart have absorbed the high-frequency top-up trip, which is the footfall driver for metro stores. Blended LFL held at 8.1% in FY26, but that is an average that likely masks metro weakness. Management has retreated rather than contested with DMart Ready exiting seven cities in the June 2026 quarter, down to 11 from 18.

• Key risk: 1) Metro LFL erosion proving structural rather than cyclical as quick commerce widens basket coverage beyond top-up 2) Cost inflation outpacing gross margin in a model with minimal buffer.
