• Urban Company is not a marketplace in the usual sense, and the take rate proves it: FY26 revenue of ₹1,556cr on net transaction value of ₹4,290cr implies roughly 36% of every transaction accruing to the platform. That is only defensible because the company controls training, pricing, quality standards and consumables rather than merely listing providers — it manufactures a standardised service experience in a category that is otherwise entirely informal, which is what customers are actually paying for.

• The core business is already profitable and the reported loss is a deliberate investment choice: excluding InstaHelp, adjusted EBITDA was positive ₹106cr in FY26, up from ₹12cr in FY25, while consolidated adjusted EBITDA was -₹129cr and net loss ₹235cr. The entire deficit is the domestic help vertical. International operations in UAE and Singapore are also profitable, with NTV up 84% in Q4 [InstaHelp investment FY27E: __; consolidated breakeven against the Q3 FY28 target: __].

• Native, the own-brand product line sold and installed through the existing service network, grew revenue 130% to ₹267cr with NTV up 122% — a vertical integration into hardware that uses the service relationship as the distribution channel, competing directly with established appliance players. This is the highest-margin part of the model and the clearest evidence that the service network is an asset capable of carrying more than services [Native share of revenue FY26: __% -> FY29E: __%].

• Key risk: 1) InstaHelp consuming core profitability for longer than guided, with breakeven targeted only by Q3 FY28 2) Supply-side dependence on service professionals, where earnings disputes or regulatory reclassification of gig work would strike directly at the cost structure.
