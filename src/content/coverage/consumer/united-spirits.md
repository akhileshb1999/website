• United Spirits operates in a category where the binding constraint is not competition but the state: manufacturers capture only ~16-20% of retail price, taxes run 60-80%, and ex-distillery prices are frozen for long stretches while excise is revised at each state budget. Combined with a total ban on advertising, this produces an industry where incumbency and portfolio depth matter far more than marketing spend, and where the only reliable margin lever is mix. Diageo's brand stable gives USL the deepest premium shelf in Indian spirits, which is the asset that matters under those rules.

• The premiumisation transition here is largely complete rather than ongoing, which changes what the next decade looks like. P&A reached ₹11,152cr of ₹12,467cr FY26 NSV — roughly 89% — with the Popular segment broadly flat. The mix-shift lever that drove the last decade is therefore close to exhausted, and further premiumisation has to come from within P&A, upgrading Prestige drinkers into Luxury, which is a slower and more contested climb than exiting mass brands was [Luxury share of P&A FY26: __% -> FY29E: __%].

• FY26 EBITDA margin of 18.4% is the highest in the listed peer set, but the quality of the recent expansion deserves scrutiny: Q4 margin improved 93bps while A&P was cut 42% sequentially to ₹300cr. Deferred brand investment flatters near-term margin and borrows from future volume, so the durable margin is the one that holds with A&P normalised [FY29E EBITDA margin with normalised A&P: __%]. Topline growth of 7.7% in FY26 reflects the structural ceiling — state-controlled pricing caps what a national premium portfolio can compound at.

• Key risk: 1) Adverse state excise or route-to-market action, as seen in Maharashtra, which hits a single geography with no offsetting pricing lever 2) A&P normalisation reversing part of the recent margin expansion.
