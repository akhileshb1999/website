• Swiggy runs the same two-business structure as Eternal but from the weaker position in both, and quick commerce is a business where relative scale determines unit economics. Instamart operates 1,143 dark stores against Blinkit's 2,243, with Q4 GOV of ₹7,881cr against Blinkit's ₹14,386cr of NOV — roughly half the scale. Since dark store costs are largely fixed, order density per store drives contribution margin, which means the larger network structurally earns better economics on the same order.

• The financial consequence is visible: Instamart's contribution margin was still negative at -1.8% in Q4 (improving 372bps YoY, with March at -1.1%), and quick commerce recorded an FY26 segment loss of ₹3,063cr, widening from ₹1,896cr in FY25. Blinkit reached positive adjusted EBITDA over the same period. Losses widening while the competitor turns profitable is the central fact of this investment case [Instamart contribution margin FY27E: __%; path to segment breakeven: __].

• Food delivery is genuinely healthy and is the reason the group is fundable: GOV grew 22.6% to ₹9,005cr in Q4 with adjusted EBITDA margin of 3.3% of GOV, delivering ₹1,041cr of positive segment result in FY26 — the only profitable segment. Management has slowed the quick commerce build to conserve capital, adding just seven dark stores in Q4 against Blinkit's 216, which improves near-term economics but concedes network scale in a business where scale compounds [dark store additions FY27E: __].

• Key risk: 1) Structural sub-scale in quick commerce, where the competitor's density advantage is self-reinforcing 2) FY26 consolidated loss of ₹4,154cr requiring the food delivery business to fund a deficit it does not currently cover.
