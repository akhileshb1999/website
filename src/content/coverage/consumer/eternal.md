• Eternal's reported revenue is close to meaningless as a growth measure: Q4 FY26 revenue rose 196.4% to ₹17,292cr almost entirely because Blinkit shifted to an inventory-led model, which grosses up revenue without changing the underlying economics. Net order value is the metric that matters, and on that basis B2C NOV grew 54%. Any analysis anchored on the revenue line will misread this business in both directions.

• The business has crossed a structural threshold: Blinkit's NOV overtook food delivery's during FY26 and reached ₹14,386cr in Q4, growing 95.4%, against food delivery NOV growth of 18.8%. Eternal is now primarily a quick commerce company that also operates a mature food delivery business — and the two have opposite financial characteristics. Food delivery is the profit engine, approaching its long-term 20% growth expectation with established margins; Blinkit is the growth engine, at ₹37cr of adjusted EBITDA on ₹14,386cr of quarterly NOV, which is barely breakeven [Blinkit adjusted EBITDA margin FY29E: __%].

• The growth algorithm is dark store additions — 216 net adds in Q4 to 2,243 stores — and the economics depend on order density per store rather than store count itself. Consolidated adjusted EBITDA margin of 5.5% of NOV reflects a mature business subsidising an expanding one; the question is at what store count and density Blinkit's contribution turns decisively positive, and whether food delivery's cash generation is sufficient to fund the land grab until then [dark stores FY29E: __; NOV per store: __].

• Key risk: 1) Quick commerce competitive intensity forcing continued discounting, deferring Blinkit's margin inflection 2) Food delivery growth decelerating, which would remove the funding source for the quick commerce build-out.
