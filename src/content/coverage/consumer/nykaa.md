• Nykaa's two businesses have opposite economics and the consolidated numbers obscure it. Beauty and personal care generated ₹9,139cr of FY26 revenue at a 9.6% EBITDA margin on net sales value, while fashion delivered GMV of ₹4,954cr but EBITDA of -₹37cr. BPC works because it is high-repeat, low-return and category-authoritative; fashion carries structurally high return rates and competes against far deeper catalogues at Myntra and Ajio, which is why the same platform capability produces very different margins.

• Consolidated FY26 profitability improved sharply — EBITDA up 59% to ₹752cr at a 7.5% margin, PAT up 183% to ₹204cr on revenue of ₹10,022cr — but a 2% net margin indicates how little of the value chain the platform retains after fulfilment and marketing. • The structural asset is the owned-brand portfolio and physical retail rather than the marketplace itself: private labels capture full margin instead of a commission, and stores serve as both acquisition and fulfilment nodes in a category where trial matters. GMV grew 28% to ₹19,963cr against revenue growth of 26%, so the mix between owned inventory and marketplace is broadly stable [owned brands share of BPC NSV: __% -> FY29E: __%].

• Key risk: 1) Quick commerce platforms absorbing replenishment beauty purchases, which are the high-frequency, low-acquisition-cost orders underpinning BPC economics 2) Fashion continuing to consume capital without reaching the scale required to compete on catalogue depth.
